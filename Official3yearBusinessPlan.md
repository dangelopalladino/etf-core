## Section 1: The Problem — What the Research Confirms

Athletic identity — defined as "the degree to which an individual identifies with the athlete role" — was formally operationalized by Brewer, Van Raalte, and Linder in 1993 and has since anchored 30+ years of peer-reviewed research on how athletes form and lose self-concept. The evidence for the clinical severity of transition disruption is now conclusive:[1][2][3]

A 2025 longitudinal study of 138 retiring elite athletes found a **32% mean reduction in athletic identity** in the three months immediately following retirement — the steepest psychological drop occurring before any institutional support could engage. A 2017 study found that the degree of pre-retirement athletic identity was a **statistically significant predictor of post-retirement anxiety symptoms**, independent of baseline anxiety levels. Depression and anxiety rates among former professional athletes range from 39–46% in peer-reviewed literature. A 2025 synthesis of 68 studies across 17,539 participants in 34 countries confirmed that stronger athletic identity reliably predicts poorer mental health during career transitions, while social support and multi-group identity promoted better outcomes.[4][5][6][7][8]

The financial consequences of identity loss compound the psychological ones. Research from the National Bureau of Economic Research documents that **15.7% of NFL players file for bankruptcy within 12 years of retirement**, with filings beginning as early as two years post-career. The identity vacuum — an athlete's failure to construct a post-sport self — is causally linked to impulsive financial behavior, social withdrawal, and career paralysis.[7][9][10]

The help-seeking gap defines the commercial opportunity: **only 10–15% of athletes with mental health challenges actively seek professional help**. The 85–90% who do not are the target market. 6identities is not competing for the clinical 15% who will eventually see a sport psychologist — it is the first contact point for the 85% who will not, providing a psychoeducational tool at a consumer price that initiates their transition identity work without requiring a clinical encounter.[11]

## Section 2: The Solution — Product Architecture

### 2.1 6identities.com — The Diagnostic Layer

**What it is:** A psychoeducational identity assessment that maps former athletes to one of six named post-sport identity archetypes, delivers a personalized "identity map," and generates a Way Forward plan. Positioned explicitly as psychoeducational (not clinical), it functions as a self-insight and personal development tool.

**Why psychoeducational positioning is correct:** This framing is not a legal workaround — it is the right product category. The most commercially successful assessment products globally (MBTI, CliftonStrengths, DiSC, StrengthsFinder) all operate in the psychoeducational space, not the clinical space. The psychoeducational frame reduces regulatory exposure, expands the accessible audience, and allows the product to operate independently of the founder's licensure status. Strong athletic identity is already documented to be associated with "negative mental health outcomes including difficulty with coping processes, negative social and emotional adjustment, and symptoms of anxiety" — meaning even a psychoeducational tool addressing this is clinically meaningful.[12][13][14]

**Product tiers:**

| Tier                | Offer                                                | Price          | Conversion Target      |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------------- |
| Free                | Archetype name + card (identity type only)           | $0             | 100% of completions    |
| Premium Result      | Archetype card + blind spots + relationship patterns | $9.99          | 3.5–5% of completions |
| Full Package        | Premium + Way Forward plan + resource guide          | $19.99–$29.99 | 1.5–2% of completions |
| Practitioner Portal | Dashboard for practitioners using 6I with clients    | $19.99/mo      | Separate B2B channel   |

**Freemium conversion benchmark:** Industry data for niche consumer psychoeducational tools shows free-to-paid conversion averaging **3.0–3.7%** at the median, reaching 5–7% for high-emotional-resonance products with strong referral loops. The 3.5% base case is used for all financial projections below.[15][16]

### 2.2 etfframework.com — The Practitioner Certification Layer

**What it is:** A practitioner certification program built on the Executable Transition Framework (ETF), delivered through a self-paced LMS (Thinkific or Kajabi) with manual case evaluation, a public verified practitioner directory, and annual listing maintenance.

**ETF is primarily a distribution multiplier for 6identities, not a standalone revenue product in Year 1.** Each certified practitioner refers clients to 6identities, increases directory search traffic, and builds the social proof needed for the B2B practitioner portal. Revenue from ETF is secondary to the flywheel effect it creates for 6I.

## Section 4: The Founder Advantage — LPCC Timeline as Strategic Asset

This is the most important section of this plan. The founder's decision to pursue LPCC licensure beginning August 2026 is not a constraint on the business — it is the business's structural moat.

### 4.1 Ohio LPCC Licensure Path

Ohio's LPCC licensure requires:[37][38][39][40]

1. **Master's degree** in counseling (60 semester hours, CACREP-accredited program preferred)
2. **LPC license first** — pass the National Counselor Examination (NCE); $80 application fee
3. **3,000 hours of post-LPC supervised clinical experience** over a minimum of 24 months, including:
   - At least 1,500 face-to-face client contact hours (50% minimum)
   - 150 hours of direct training supervision from an LPCC-S
4. **NCMHCE exam** — National Clinical Mental Health Counseling Examination; $100 application fee
5. **Independent licensure** — full LPCC, authorized to independently diagnose and treat mental and emotional disorders

**Realistic timeline:**

- August 2026: Begin graduate counseling program
- May/June 2028: Graduate with master's (estimated)
- Fall 2028: Apply for and receive LPC license
- Fall 2028–Fall 2030: 3,000 hours supervised post-LPC clinical experience
- 2030–2031: LPCC independent licensure

This means the founder will be an **LPC (supervised practice)** by late 2028 and an **independent LPCC** by 2030–2031. The 3-year business plan below runs to the LPC milestone. The full clinical scope — including use of 6identities in a licensed counseling context — unlocks at the LPC stage.

### 4.3 Scope of Practice Guardrails (Pre-Licensure)

Before LPC licensure, the founder must operate 6identities and ETF strictly within the **psychoeducational and coaching** scope. This means:

**Permitted (no license required):**

- Developing and selling psychoeducational assessment tools
- Creating and selling online courses and certification programs
- Coaching and consultation (not clinical counseling)
- Research and writing about identity transition
- Building technology tools for practitioner workflows
- Marketing and distributing tools to athletes as self-help resources

**Not permitted (requires licensure):**

- Diagnosing or treating mental or emotional disorders
- Providing clinical counseling services
- Holding oneself out as a licensed counselor
- Using the tools in a way that implies clinical diagnosis

**Key legal note for online courses and assessments:** For most practitioners operating online courses as a separate non-clinical business entity, courses function as psychoeducational content rather than clinical services — a distinction upheld in practice and legal review. Maintaining 6identities and ETF as separate business entities from any future clinical practice is essential for liability separation. Consult with a business attorney in Ohio before launching and when transitioning to clinical practice.[43]

### 5.2 The Flywheel

The self-reinforcing growth loop is:

1. Consumer athletes complete the 6I assessment → see "Find an ETF-Certified Practitioner" CTA → click to directory
2. Directory traffic creates referral value for certified practitioners → practitioners renew $500–$1,000/yr directory listing
3. Certified practitioners refer clients to 6I assessment → completions grow organically
4. Completion volume and practitioner testimonials → academic and institutional credibility → validity study → institutional channel opens
5. Institutional channel sends large cohorts through 6I → conversion volume grows → repeat

**This flywheel does not require paid acquisition.** The first 90 days are about igniting the flywheel with enough volume (200–500 completions) that the other components have something to amplify.

### 5.3 First 100 Customers Playbook

**6identities B2C:**

1. Email 5–10 N4A member institutions (not just individuals) with the free assessment offer
2. Post 3 pieces of Reel-format content on Instagram targeting the "athlete identity crisis" emotional search: "I don't know who I am without my sport" — this is the exact search query that already has peer-reviewed academic literature behind it[5][4]
3. Email LPCC cohort classmates: "I built a free assessment for athletes going through identity transition — can you share it with any former athletes you know?"
4. One LinkedIn post written as a personal essay: "The one thing no one told me about life after sport" — targeted at former athletes and coaches

**ETF Early Adopter Cohort:**

1. Build a spreadsheet of 200 practitioners from AASP CMPC directory, Psychology Today sport psychology listings, and LinkedIn
2. Prioritize practitioners who post about athlete transition, identity, or mental performance
3. Send 20 cold outreach emails: "I'm building the first practitioner certification specifically for athlete identity transition. I'm offering a founding member cohort at $499 — your feedback shapes the final program."
4. Run the first cohort as a live 4-week workshop (Zoom + shared materials) before the LMS is fully built — this generates revenue and testimonials simultaneously while building the product

## Section 8: Validity & Academic Strategy

The single most important infrastructure investment over the 3-year horizon is the psychometric validation of the 6I framework. Without it, the institutional channel remains closed and ETF credibility is limited for licensed practitioners.

**The Validation Roadmap:**

| Phase   | Timeline                  | Action                                                                                                                                                          | Output                                     |
| ------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Phase 1 | May–August 2026          | Contact Ohio State Sport Psychology faculty (Dr. Jamey Houle, Director of Sport Psychology and Wellness[41], or Dr. Sam Davis, Research Coordinator[41])        | Research collaboration letter of intent    |
| Phase 2 | August–November 2026     | Frame pilot as graduate thesis or independent research project; submit IRB application through university                                                       | IRB approval                               |
| Phase 3 | November 2026–April 2027 | Administer both AIMS and 6I instrument to 100–200 former athletes (recruited through university athletic department and UNCG/Illinois State/Michigan programs) | Raw dataset                                |
| Phase 4 | April–July 2027          | Run concurrent validity analysis: Pearson correlation, factor analysis, group difference tests between archetypes on AIMS subscales                             | Statistical results                        |
| Phase 5 | August–December 2027     | Write and submit to peer-reviewed journal (Journal of Sport Psychology, The Sport Psychologist, or Journal of Applied Sport Psychology)                         | Preprint or under-review status            |
| Phase 6 | 2028                      | Publication or accepted manuscript                                                                                                                              | Cite on all 6I and ETF marketing materials |

**The AIMS context:** The Athletic Identity Measurement Scale was originally validated on samples of 350 intercollegiate student-athletes across Division I and Division III. A third-generation quantitative assessment published in 2022 confirmed AIMS's unidimensional structure and two subscales measuring athletic identity prominence and self-worth contingency. The 6I validity study should reference this literature directly and position the 6I archetypes as a *complementary framework* that adds applied interpretive value to the dimensional scores AIMS produces — not a replacement for AIMS, but a practitioner-facing translation layer.[1]

**CEU positioning for ETF:** AASP CMPCs must earn 75 CEUs over a 5-year recertification cycle, including credits in specific required categories. The ETF certification program can be positioned as a CEU-eligible professional development activity — creating demand-pull among the ~1,500 CMPCs already seeking recertification content. This requires AASP CEU provider application; confirm current requirements and submission process directly with AASP.[17][50]

## Conclusion: Why This Works

The convergence of three factors makes this plan viable and defensible:

**1. Genuine white space.** The peer-reviewed literature on athletic identity transition spans 30+ years and thousands of studies. No consumer product has translated this literature into a self-serve, affordable identity diagnostic. The white space is not an opinion — it is documented in the absence of existing tools.[54][2][1]

**2. The founder is building the credential while building the product.** By the time LPCC licensure is complete, the founder will have a validated psychometric instrument, a certified practitioner network, recurring revenue, and academic credibility that most licensed practitioners never accumulate. The business is not a distraction from the clinical career — it is the clinical career's commercial infrastructure, built ahead of schedule.

**3. The model is self-funding.** The total cost to launch is under $2,500. The first ETF cohort alone ($499 × 15 practitioners) returns $7,485 — covering all launch costs and funding 12+ months of operations. No investor is required to prove the model, and no debt is required to survive the validation period.

The risk is not the market. The risk is not the competition. The risk is execution discipline: launching before August, building the validity study into the academic program from day one, and treating the flywheel (6I completions → ETF directory → practitioner referrals) as the engine that requires feeding in Year 1 before it runs on its own.
