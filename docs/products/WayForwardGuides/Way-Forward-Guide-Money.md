# Way Forward Guide™: The Money Guide
**Getting financially stable when the contract is gone.**

The contract ended. The stipend stopped. The signing bonus is somewhere in the past.

Most athletes were never taught to think about money outside of what sport paid them. The financial infrastructure during a career — agents, advisors, guaranteed contracts, per diems, equipment covered — made it possible to be excellent at sport and financially illiterate at the same time. That combination works fine while the career is running. It becomes a problem the day it stops.

This is not a personal finance guide. It is a financial floor guide. The goal here is not wealth-building. It is stability — the specific kind of financial stability that lets you do the identity work without fear running every decision.

Fear-driven financial decisions in transition are almost always wrong. This guide eliminates the fear first.

---

## Section 1: The Financial Reality of Transition

Most athletes leave sport in one of three financial positions. Know which one you are in before reading the rest of this guide.

### Position 1: The runway exists

You have savings, investments, or ongoing income that covers your baseline expenses for at least 12 months. The financial pressure is real but not acute. Your job in transition is to not blow the runway on comfort spending that mimics the lifestyle of your playing days.

The trap here is not destitution. It is lifestyle creep that burns the runway faster than expected, compressing the time available to build something real before fear takes over the decision-making.

### Position 2: The runway is short

You have three to six months of expenses covered, maybe less. The financial pressure is acute and it is affecting every other part of the transition. The identity work is harder when the bank account is dropping every month. The career question feels more urgent than it actually is, which produces bad decisions made fast.

If this is your position, Section 3 of this guide is the most important section. Read it first.

### Position 3: The runway is gone

Income stopped and the gap between expenses and available funds is already producing real consequences. This is a financial emergency, and financial emergencies require a different sequencing than transition work. The Block List entry here is not an obstacle — it is the primary issue. Get financial triage done before anything else in this guide.

## Section 2: The Income Floor

Before you can build anything, you need to know the number.

The income floor is the monthly amount that covers your actual non-negotiable expenses — housing, food, transportation, insurance, debt obligations — with nothing left over for lifestyle. Not what you want to live on. Not what you were used to living on. The floor.

Most athletes in transition have never calculated this number because they never had to. The salary covered everything and then some. Calculate it now.

> **The income floor exercise:** List every fixed monthly expense. List every variable monthly expense at its realistic average, not its best-case version. Add them. That number is your floor. Everything above it is lifestyle. Everything at or below it is survival. You need to know the difference.

Write the number down. This is the only financial target that matters right now. Not six figures. Not financial freedom. The floor.

Once you know the floor, the question changes from 'how do I get rich?' to 'how do I cover this specific number?' That question has real answers.

## Section 3: The Fastest Path to the Floor

This section is for athletes in Position 2 or 3. If you are in Position 1, read it anyway — the principles apply at every runway length.

The fastest path to the income floor is almost never the most interesting path. It is the most available path. The goal here is not career alignment. It is financial stability that buys time for career alignment.

### What athletes can monetize immediately

**Coaching and instruction.** You have a skill at the highest level of development most people will ever encounter. Private instruction, skills clinics, youth coaching, team consulting — these can generate income inside 30 days with no infrastructure beyond a phone and a willingness to show up. This is not a career. It is a bridge.

**Physical training.** Personal training, group fitness instruction, corporate wellness programs. The same logic applies. You know how to train people. That knowledge has immediate market value even if it is not where you are building long-term.

**Consulting inside your sport's ecosystem.** Brands, media companies, startups adjacent to your sport need people who understand the culture from the inside. This is not front-office work. It is short-term, project-based consulting that pays without requiring a full-time commitment or a long hiring process.

**Sales roles with athletic compensation structures.** Commission-only or heavily commission-weighted sales roles pay like sport — nothing guaranteed, everything tied to performance. Athletes are often better at these roles than they expect because the competitive structure is familiar. The scoreboard is just denominated in dollars instead of points.

> None of these is the answer to the career question. All of them are valid answers to the income floor question. Getting to the floor buys the time to find the real answer.

## Section 4: The Financial Block List

Money in transition produces specific blocks that are different from the general Block List categories. Name them before they run the decision-making.

### The lifestyle block

Spending at the rate of the playing days on a fraction of the playing income. This is the most common financial block in transition and the most socially invisible — no one tells you your spending is a problem because you used to be able to afford it. The income floor calculation exposes this. If your monthly spend is significantly above your floor, the gap is the block.

### The identity block

Money from sport felt earned. Money from anything else feels like settling. This block produces paralysis — athletes who will not take available income because it does not feel worthy of who they are. The reframe: the income floor is not your ceiling. It is the foundation. Taking bridge income while building something real is not settling. It is sequencing.

### The agent block

For years, someone else managed the financial relationship. Contracts were negotiated. Endorsement terms were handled. Tax situations were addressed by someone else. In transition, that infrastructure is gone and many athletes have never built the basic financial literacy the infrastructure was covering. This block is solvable — a fee-only financial advisor who works with athletes in transition costs money but costs less than the decisions made without one.

### The comparison block

Other athletes are still earning. Former teammates are in lucrative post-career roles. Social media makes everyone else's financial situation look resolved. The comparison block is purely cognitive — it produces shame, urgency, and bad decisions. The only financial comparison that matters is your floor versus your current income. Everything else is noise.

## Section 5: Income Aligned with Core Code™

Once the floor is covered, the question shifts from survival to direction. This is where Core Code™ becomes a financial filter, not just a career filter.

The income aligned with Core Code™ is not always the highest-paying option. It is the option you will sustain, build on, and not quietly sabotage because it is grinding against who you actually are.

### The Core Code™ income filter

Before pursuing any income opportunity above the floor, ask three questions:

1. Does this role give my dominant engine what it needs? (A Sovereign needs autonomy. A Rival needs a scoreboard. A Bonded needs relational depth. A Guardian needs visible impact. A Pioneer needs something to build.)
2. Can I see a path from this role to something I would actually want in three years, or does this role dead-end?
3. Am I pursuing this because it fits or because it is available and I am afraid?

The third question is the most important. Fear-driven income decisions in transition feel like pragmatism. They are usually the identity block in disguise.

## Section 6: The Financial Move™ for the Next 90 Days

Two Moves™. Run them in parallel.

**Move™ 1: Calculate and document your income floor.** Not an estimate. The actual number, with every expense listed. Done by day 7.

**Move™ 2: Identify three specific ways to reach your income floor within 90 days using skills you already have.** Not ideas. Specific: who would pay you, for what, and how much. Done by day 30.

Everything else in the financial picture — investments, long-term planning, wealth-building — comes after the floor is stable. The floor first. Always.

> The financial question in transition is not 'how do I get back to where I was?' It is 'what is the number I need to stop making decisions from fear?' Find that number. Cover it. Then build.
