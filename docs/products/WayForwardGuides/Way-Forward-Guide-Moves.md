# Way Forward Guide™: The Moves Guide
**90-day execution when the playbook is yours to write.**

---

6 IDENTITIES® | WAY FORWARD GUIDE™

---

Athletes understand execution. That is not the problem.

The problem is executing without someone else's playbook. In sport, the game plan was given to you. You executed it. You were held accountable to it in real time. The feedback was immediate and the stakes were clear.

In transition, you are the head coach. You write the playbook, call the plays, run the drills, and hold yourself accountable. For athletes who were elite executors, that shift is harder than it looks.

This guide teaches you to write the playbook and run it.

---

## Section 1: The Difference Between Goals and Moves™

Goals are directional. Moves™ are executable.

Goals say 'get in better shape.' Moves™ say 'lift three times per week for 12 consecutive weeks and add 10 pounds to my squat by day 84.' Goals say 'build my network.' Moves™ say 'have six conversations with people outside sport in the next 60 days and follow up on each within 72 hours.'

The difference is not ambition. It is precision. Goals produce motivation. Moves™ produce progress.

> A Move™ is complete when you can point to the specific thing that exists now that did not exist before you ran it. If you cannot name that thing, you do not have a Move™. You have a goal with Move™ language.

---

## Section 2: The Move™ Setting Formula

Every Move™ has four parts. All four must be present. If any one is missing, the Move™ is not ready to run.

### Part 1: The specific action

Not a direction. A verb with an object. Not 'pursue a new career' but 'have conversations with four people currently doing work aligned with my Core Code™.' Not 'get stronger' but 'complete 36 scheduled lift sessions.' Not 'build a support system' but 'ask one person to serve as my Anchor for the next 90 days.'

### Part 2: The deadline

Not 'by the end of the quarter.' A date. Day 30, day 60, day 84, or a specific calendar date. Deadlines without dates are suggestions. You would not show up to practice 'sometime this week.'

### Part 3: The completion condition

How will you know when the Move™ is done? What exists that did not exist before? This is the scorecard. If you cannot define done, you cannot cross the finish line because you cannot find it.

### Part 4: The accountability structure

Who knows about this Move™? Who will ask about it at day 30? The Anchor holds this. Without it, the Move™ lives only in your head and competes with every other thing in your head.

> Full formula: I will [specific action] by [specific date]. I will know it is done when [specific outcome]. [Name] will ask me about it at the Check-In™ on [specific date].

## Section 3: The Six™ Coverage Check

Your 90-day Moves™ should span the six areas of your life, not just career. The six areas are: career and income, relationships and people, physical health and body, mental and emotional state, community and belonging, and purpose and direction.

Athletes in transition over-index on career because career is the most visible gap. But the athlete who lands a job while everything else is collapsing is not in transition. They are in a different kind of stuck.

**The Coverage Check:**

List your current three to five Moves™. Next to each one, write which of the six areas it addresses. If four Moves™ are all in career and income, that is not a balanced 90-day cycle. Add one Move™ in the area most neglected.

This is not about equal time across all six. It is about intentional coverage. You know which areas are most depleted. The Coverage Check makes that visible so you can address it deliberately.

## Section 4: The Check-In™

The Check-In™ is the weekly review that keeps the 90-day cycle running. Without it, Moves™ drift. Deadlines slip. The cycle ends without a close.

Block 30 minutes on the same day and time every week. That day and time do not change. Treat it the way you treated a film session. You do not skip it because you are tired. You do not move it because something else came up. It runs.

**The Check-In™ agenda (30 minutes):**

1. **Dashboard review (5 minutes).** How did the key metrics look this week? Not analysis — just naming. Are things moving?
2. **Move™ check (15 minutes).** For each active Move™: what progress was made? What got in the way? What is the next specific action before the next Check-In™?
3. **The Block List (5 minutes).** What is currently in the way? Name it. Do not solve it yet. Just name it.
4. **Next week's one thing (5 minutes).** If you could only do one thing before the next Check-In™ that would move the most important Move™ forward, what is it? Name it. Write it down. Tell the Anchor.

The first few Check-Ins™ will feel rough. The data will be sparse. The Moves™ will be fuzzy. Run it anyway. The discipline of the Check-In™ is worth more than the quality of the data in the early weeks.

## Section 5: The Anchor Relationship

The Anchor is not a coach. They are not a therapist. They are not a cheerleader.

The Anchor is the person who asks one question at every Check-In™: did you do what you said you were going to do? If yes, the next question is: what is the next Move™? If no, the next question is: what got in the way?

The Anchor does not fix the answer. They hold the question. That is the whole job.

**Finding your Anchor:**

Look for someone who will not let you off the hook. Not because they are harsh — because they respect your goals enough to hold you to them. The Anchor who says 'no worries, transition is hard' every time you miss a deadline is not an Anchor. That is sympathy. You need accountability.

The Anchor can be paid (a coach or practitioner using the ETF™ system) or unpaid (a Build Supporter with the right disposition). The qualification is not credentials. It is willingness to ask the hard question every week without softening it.

> Tell your Anchor what you are building. Give them your three Moves™. Set the Check-In™ date. That conversation, today, is the single most high-leverage action in this guide.

## Section 6: The Reboot™

Every 90 days, the cycle closes. You run a Reboot™.

The Reboot™ is a structured review of the cycle that just ended. Not a celebration. Not a self-assessment. A data read.

**The Reboot™ questions:**

1. Which Moves™ were completed? What specifically happened?
2. Which Moves™ were not completed? What got in the way?
3. What did this cycle teach me about my Core Code™ and what I am actually building?
4. What one thing from this cycle will I carry into the next one?
5. What are the three Moves™ for the next 90 days?

The Reboot™ closes the old cycle and opens the new one. This is periodization applied to life. Build, peak, recover, reboot. The same rhythm your body already knows.

## Section 7: When the Moves™ Are Not Moving

Every athlete has hit a wall. You know what that feels like. The body is not responding. The training is not translating. The game plan is not working. The instinct is to train harder. Sometimes the right answer is to change the game plan.

When Moves™ are not moving after two consecutive Check-Ins™, ask three questions before adding effort:

- Is the Move™ actually specific enough? (Vague Moves™ produce vague progress.)
- Is the obstacle named? (Something in the Block List may be running interference. Name it before pushing harder.)
- Is the Move™ aligned with Core Code™? (If the Move™ is in service of claimed values rather than Core Code™, it will resist execution at the level of the body, not just the mind.)

If all three answers check out and progress is still stalled, the Move™ needs a smaller first step. Find the version of this Move™ that you can complete in the next seven days with the resources you currently have. That version is the real next Move™.

## Section 8: Your Move™ for Right Now

The best time to set a Move™ is when you have the most clarity about what matters. That moment is this one.

Before you close this guide, do these three things:

1. Write one Move™ using the full formula (specific action, deadline, completion condition, accountability structure).
2. Text or call your Anchor now and tell them the Move™.
3. Set the first Check-In™ date in your calendar before you do anything else today.

The 90-day clock starts when you name the Move™ and tell someone. Not when you feel ready. Not when the situation is perfect. Now.
