# Way Forward Guide™: The People Guide
**Building the team after the locker room disappears.**

---

6 IDENTITIES® | WAY FORWARD GUIDE™

---

The locker room did not just give you teammates. It gave you a complete social infrastructure. Daily proximity to people who shared your values and your intensity. Accountability without having to ask for it. A shared enemy, a shared goal, and a shared language. You did not have to build any of that. It was handed to you the day you made the team.

When the career ended, it disappeared. Not gradually. All at once.

The athletes who struggle most in transition are often the ones who had the best locker rooms. The higher the quality of what sport gave them socially, the larger the gap when it was gone.

This guide closes that gap.

---

## Section 1: What You Are Actually Missing

It is not the teammates. It is the structure the teammates provided.

Sport gave you five things that most people never have simultaneously: identity (who you are), community (who you belong to), structure (what you do every day), neurochemistry (the dopamine of competition and achievement), and purpose (why any of it matters).

When those five collapse at once, the instinct is to go find people to fill the void. Make connections. Network. Meet people. The problem is that what you are missing is not people. It is a framework for the people to function inside.

> You are not lonely because you don't know anyone. You are isolated because the structure that made your relationships meaningful no longer exists. The fix is not more contacts. It is a new structure.

---

## Section 2: The Three Roles That Replace the Locker Room

In sport, the support structure was automatic. Outside it, you build it deliberately. The people who carry your transition fall into three categories. You need at least one person in each.

### The Foundation Holder

This is the person who knew you before the career made you an athlete. A parent, a childhood friend, a sibling, a coach from before the sport consumed everything. Their job is not to understand the transition. Their job is to remember who you were before it. In moments of identity confusion, the Foundation Holder is the anchor to the person that existed before sport.

Do you have one? Name them now. If the answer is no, the Zero-Network Protocol in the full ETF™ system addresses this specifically.

### The Build Supporter

This is the person who believes in what you are building now, not what you did before. They do not need to understand athletic identity transition. They need to be interested in you as a person with a direction. They ask about your Moves™. They notice when you are stuck. They celebrate specific progress, not general encouragement.

You need at least two Build Supporters. They are different from cheerleaders. Cheerleaders say 'you've got this.' Build Supporters say 'what happened with that conversation you said you were going to have?'

### The Anchor

The Anchor is the person who holds you to your 90-day Moves™. They do not advise. They do not coach. They ask one question at the Check-In™: did you do what you said you were going to do? If yes, what is the next Move™? If no, what got in the way?

The Anchor can be a Build Supporter, but they do not have to be. The key qualification is that they will not let you off the hook. A friend who says 'no worries, life gets busy' when you miss a Move™ deadline is not an Anchor. An Anchor says 'we talked about this. What happened?'

## Section 3: The People Audit

Before you can build the team, you have to see what is already on the field. The People Audit does that.

Pull out a piece of paper. Draw four columns.

### Column 1: Who builds me?

Name every person who, after time with them, leaves you with more clarity, more energy, or more progress than before. These are your Build Supporters. You want more of them.

### Column 2: Who drains me?

Name every person who, after time with them, leaves you more scattered, more stuck, or more exhausted. These are Build Blockers. You do not have to end every relationship. But you need to be honest about the cost.

### Column 3: Who knew me before sport?

Name everyone in your life who knew you before you were an athlete. These are your potential Foundation Holders. If this column is empty, the transition is harder than it needs to be. The work here is to find or rebuild one of these relationships.

### Column 4: Who holds me accountable?

Name anyone in your life who will ask you in 30 days whether you did the thing you said you were going to do. If this column is empty, you do not yet have an Anchor.

> The audit is not about cutting people. It is about seeing the field clearly. You cannot build a team you cannot name.

## Section 4: The CEO Standard

Athletes know what team standards feel like. The locker room had them. The coaching staff enforced them. Everyone understood what being on the team required.

In transition, you are the CEO of your own life. That means you set the standard for the people around you. Not out of arrogance. Out of design.

The CEO standard does not mean demanding perfection from everyone in your life. It means communicating clearly what you need from the people who are supposed to support your build. A CEO does not run an organization out of obligation or history. A CEO builds a team that serves the mission.

**The three-part communication:**

1. This is what I am building. (Your direction. Your 90-day Move™. The specific thing you are working toward.)
2. This is what support looks like. (What you need from this person specifically. Not general encouragement.)
3. This is how I will know it is working. (The specific thing you are asking them to do or not do.)

Most athletes in transition never have this conversation. They assume the people who love them understand what they need. They do not. The conversation is not a demand. It is a blueprint.

## Section 5: When the Network Is Empty

Some athletes come out of sport with a full roster. Some come out with almost no one.

If the transition has been isolating, if the relationships inside sport did not survive the career ending, if you look at the four columns in the People Audit and most of them are blank — this is not a character flaw. It is a structural problem.

Sport was the room where you met people. That room is closed. You need new rooms.

### The Zero-Network Protocol (three-Cycle version):

**Cycle 1:** One new room per week. Not a networking event. A recurring environment where the same people show up — a class, a league, a group, a regular meetup — organized around something aligned with your Core Code™. You are not going to make friends. You are going to find the room.

**Cycle 2:** One real conversation per week inside the room you chose. Not small talk. One conversation that lasts more than ten minutes and goes past weather and surface logistics.

**Cycle 3:** One genuine ask. Ask one person from your room for something specific. Coffee. Input. An introduction. A conversation about their work. This is the move that separates the people who rebuild community from the ones who stay observers.

> You are not looking for teammates. You are looking for people who want to build something. That is the Core Code™ sorting function in action. The right rooms surface the right people.

---

## Section 6: Your People Move™ for the Next 90 Days

Pick one of these. Do it. This week.

- Name your Foundation Holder and reach out to them today. Not to talk about the transition. Just to reconnect.
- Identify one person currently in your life who qualifies as a Build Supporter and ask them explicitly to hold you to one 90-day Move™.
- Find one recurring room aligned with your Core Code™ and commit to attending for six consecutive weeks before evaluating.
- Have the CEO standard conversation with one person who is currently supposed to be supporting your build but does not know what that means.

The locker room is not coming back. But a team can be built. It is built one deliberate move at a time.
