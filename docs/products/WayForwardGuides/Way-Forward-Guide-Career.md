## Section 1: The Career Mistake Most Athletes Make

They chase the closest thing to sport.

Coaching. Broadcasting. Front office. Sports business. Youth training. These are not wrong choices. Some athletes belong exactly there. But many athletes land in sport-adjacent work because it is familiar, not because it fits. The locker room language is still spoken. The status cues still make sense. The identity question gets avoided for another few years.

The tell: a quiet unease that follows you even after the transition looks successful. A sense that you are adjacent to the right life but not quite in it.

> The question is not whether working in sport is good or bad. The question is whether it is a conscious choice aligned with your Core Code™ or a comfort zone wearing the clothes of a plan.

Before you apply for anything, read the next section.

## Section 2: Your Core Code™ Is the Only Career Filter That Works

Most career advice tells you to match your skills to a job description. That approach produces technically correct answers to the wrong question. Skills are what you can do. Core Code™ is what you cannot stop being. The career built on skills but against Core Code™ will produce results and nothing that feels like it matters.

You have your Core Code™ Archetype from the assessment. Use it here.

### If you are a Sovereign (Autonomous):

Your engine runs on independence. You will suffocate in a traditional employment structure inside eighteen months. Look for: business ownership, commission-only sales with uncapped upside, consulting, professional services, any role with a clear individual scorecard and no micromanagement. What kills you: committee culture, approval layers, open-door management that turns into constant check-ins.

### If you are a Guardian (Servant):

Your engine runs on service and impact. You will feel empty in roles that are technically excellent but organizationally inward-facing. Look for: nonprofit leadership, education, healthcare administration, community development, any role where you can point to a specific person who is better because of your work. What kills you: pure profit-center roles with no human outcome visible.

### If you are a Rival (Competitor):

Your engine runs on the scoreboard. You need a measurable version of winning. Look for: sales, trading, competitive media, anything with a clear ranking, quota, or record to break. Your highest risk is substituting the career scoreboard for sport's neurochemical hit through gambling, risk-seeking, or substance use. Build a career scoreboard before the brain finds one for you.

### If you are a Bonded (Connector):

Your engine runs on relational depth and trust. You will not survive transactional work cultures. Look for: relationship-driven businesses, partnerships, team leadership, any role where the quality of the relationship is the product. What kills you: remote-first, zero-face-time cultures that treat people as task units.

### If you are a Pioneer (Creator):

Your engine runs on building and legacy. You start fast and burn hot. Look for: founding roles, early-stage environments, product creation, any context where your fingerprint is visible on what gets built. What kills you: maintenance work. You are not a manager of stable systems. You are a builder of new ones.

## Section 3: The Transferable Assets Inventory

You built real skills inside sport that transfer directly outside it. Most athletes cannot name them because they were never labeled. This section names them.

Work through this list. Check what applies. The goal is a concrete inventory, not a resume.

### Performance under pressure

You have performed in front of crowds with real stakes on the line. Most professionals have never experienced that. You know the difference between the version of yourself in practice and the version under pressure, and you have trained the gap between them. Name the specific high-stakes moments you have performed through. Those are evidence.

### Coachability

You received real-time, often blunt feedback from coaches and adjusted. You know how to take correction without it becoming an identity crisis. This is rarer in the professional world than any technical skill. Name the coaches who pushed you hardest. Name what changed.

### Team function

You know what it means to subordinate personal performance to collective outcome. You know what a functional locker room feels like from the inside and what a broken one costs. You can read a team's culture faster than most executives because you lived inside one.

### Periodization and recovery

You understand the difference between building, peaking, and recovering. Most professionals run at full output until they burn out. You were trained to cycle. That discipline is a career asset.

### Film study / pattern recognition

You watched hours of tape, identified patterns, and adjusted accordingly. In business, that is called competitive intelligence, market analysis, and iteration. Same skill, different terminology.

> Write down ten things you got better at inside sport that have nothing to do with the sport itself. Those ten things are your transferable asset list.

## Section 4: The Career Audit

Before you build forward, name where you are. Honestly.

**Answer these five questions in writing. Not in your head. On paper.**

1. What does my current career situation actually look like? (Title, income, day-to-day reality, not the version I tell people at dinner.)
2. If I had to rate my Core Code™ alignment in this role from 1 to 10, what is the number? What specifically is misaligned?
3. What am I avoiding? What would I pursue if the outcome was guaranteed?
4. What do I know how to do that I have not yet sold? (Refer to your transferable assets inventory.)
5. If I had to generate income in 90 days using only what I already know, what are the three most realistic options?

The answers to questions 4 and 5 are your starting point. The rest of this guide builds from there.

## Section 5: The 90-Day Career Move™

You do not need a five-year plan. You need a 90-day Move™.

A Move™ is a specific, concrete action with a measurable outcome and a 90-day deadline. It is not a goal. Goals are directional. Moves™ are executable. Goals say 'get a better job.' Moves™ say 'identify eight companies aligned with my Core Code™ and have an exploratory conversation with one contact inside each by day 60.'

**The Career Move™ formula:**

> I will [specific action] by [specific date] and I will know it is done when [specific measurable outcome].

**Examples:**

- I will complete the Transferable Assets Inventory in full and send it to two people who know my work well, asking them to add what I missed. Done by day 14.
- I will identify six roles or business categories that match my Core Code™ Archetype and research one person currently doing each. Done by day 30.
- I will have one real conversation (not a LinkedIn message exchange) with a person outside sport who is doing work that interests me. Done by day 45.
- I will define my income floor — the number I need to not be afraid — and identify two concrete ways to reach it in the next six months. Done by day 60.

Pick one. Set the deadline. Tell someone who will ask you about it. That is the Move™.

## Section 6: What the Next 90 Days Actually Looks Like

The career question does not get answered in a sitting. It gets answered in a cycle.

Cycle 1 is for mapping. You are not choosing a career yet. You are building the data set the choice will eventually come from. Every conversation, every role you research, every transferable skill you name adds to that data set. The map gets clearer with use.

The athletes who figure this out fastest are the ones who move while the map is incomplete. They do not wait for certainty. They take the next specific action and adjust based on what they learn.

Your engine ran on practice. This is practice.
